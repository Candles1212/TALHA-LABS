UNDERSTANDING THE DEPARTMENTS

Different tests answer different questions.

Your doctor chooses tests based on your health history and clinical needs. These short descriptions are general information only.

HEMATOLOGY

Blood cell analysis

Hematology examines blood cells and related components. A CBC is a commonly requested test in this department.

BIOCHEMISTRY

Chemical markers

Biochemistry tests measure substances in blood and other samples, including markers used to assess liver, kidney and metabolic health.

SEROLOGY

Antigens and antibodies

Serology tests look for selected antigens or antibodies and may support a clinician's assessment of certain infections.

⚗DEPARTMENT 01Clinical BiochemistryExplore liver, kidney, glucose, lipid and metabolic markers＋

Clinical biochemistry measures chemical substances in blood, urine and other body fluids. These measurements can help clinicians assess organ function, metabolism and changes over time.

Common test groups

Liver Function Tests (LFTs)

May include ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, albumin and other markers depending on the panel. They help assess patterns related to liver-cell irritation, bile flow and protein production.

Useful question: Which exact markers are included in this laboratory's LFT panel?
Kidney Function Tests (KFTs)

Common measurements include creatinine, urea and electrolytes. Estimated filtration measures may be calculated by a clinician using results and patient information.

Useful question: Does the requested panel include electrolytes and eGFR?
Blood glucose

Glucose testing measures the amount of sugar in the blood at the time of collection. Fasting and random glucose tests answer different clinical questions.

Useful question: Should this sample be fasting, and for how long?
HbA1c

HbA1c reflects average blood-glucose exposure over roughly the previous 2–3 months. It is used by clinicians in diabetes screening and monitoring.

Preparation: Fasting is generally not required, but follow the requesting clinician's instructions.
Lipid profile

Usually reports total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, LDL cholesterol and triglycerides. The result helps clinicians evaluate cardiovascular risk alongside other factors.

Useful question: Does my clinician require fasting for this particular lipid test?
Electrolytes

Measurements such as sodium, potassium and chloride help assess fluid and electrolyte balance. Interpretation depends on symptoms, medicines and other results.

Useful question: Are electrolytes included in my requested KFT or ordered separately?

How to understand a report

Compare each value with the reference interval printed by the performing laboratory. A result outside that interval does not automatically establish a disease; age, symptoms, medicines, sample quality and clinical history matter.

Before your sample

  • Confirm fasting requirements for the exact tests ordered.
  • Tell the healthcare professional about medicines and supplements; do not stop them unless your clinician instructs you.
  • Ask when and how results will be delivered.
🩸DEPARTMENT 02HematologyExplore blood cells, haemoglobin and clotting＋

Hematology studies blood and blood-forming tissues. Blood-cell measurements can help clinicians investigate issues such as anaemia, infection, inflammation and bleeding or clotting concerns.

Complete Blood Count (CBC)

Typically includes red blood cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit, white blood cell count and platelet count. Some reports also include red-cell indices and a differential count.

Haemoglobin

Haemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. Its level is interpreted with other CBC values and the patient's clinical context.

White blood cells

White blood cells are part of the immune system. A differential may show different white-cell types and can support further clinical assessment.

Platelets

Platelets help blood clot. Their count is considered with symptoms, medicines and other coagulation investigations when needed.

What can affect results?

Hydration, recent illness, medicines, pregnancy, altitude and many other factors may influence blood counts. A clinician interprets patterns rather than relying on a single number.

Questions to ask

  • Does the CBC include a differential count?
  • Is a peripheral blood film required by the clinician?
  • When should a repeat test or clinical review be considered?
◎DEPARTMENT 03Serology & ImmunologyExplore antibodies, antigens and immune responses＋

Serology tests examine components of the immune response, often by detecting specific antibodies or antigens. The meaning of a result depends on the test method, timing and clinical context.

Antibodies

Antibodies are proteins made by the immune system in response to a target. Different antibody classes can provide different clues, but do not always prove active infection.

Antigens

Antigens are target substances that some tests detect directly. A positive or negative result must be interpreted using the specific assay and its limitations.

Screening tests

Some tests are designed for screening and may require confirmatory testing. Follow the clinician's advice about next steps.

Important to know

Test sensitivity, specificity, the time since exposure or symptoms, and prior vaccination or infection can affect interpretation. A laboratory result alone is not a complete diagnosis.

◈DEPARTMENT 04Hormones & EndocrinologyExplore thyroid and other hormone tests＋

Hormones are chemical messengers that help regulate metabolism, growth, reproduction and many body processes. Hormone results may vary with time of day, medicines, age and other factors.

Thyroid profile (TSH, T4, T3)

TSH is commonly used to assess thyroid regulation. Free or total T4 and T3 may be added depending on the clinical question.

Reproductive hormones

Tests such as oestradiol, progesterone, testosterone, LH and FSH are selected according to the clinical question. Timing within a menstrual cycle may matter for some tests.

Vitamin D

The 25-hydroxy vitamin D test is commonly used to assess vitamin D status. Interpretation depends on clinical context and the laboratory's reference guidance.

Before testing

Ask whether the sample needs a particular collection time or cycle day, and tell your clinician about hormone medicines or supplements.
